    01 / The visitor journey

    From “will it fit?”
    to “this feels right.”

    Open a ready-made sample immediately, or add your own room with guided photo setup. The useful result is a room you can keep adjusting.

    1

    Bring a room

    Choose a sample or add photos. Tell us whether you are furnishing, filling gaps or rearranging.

    2

    Check the essentials

    Review the room and furniture draft. Confirm the dimensions, doors and windows that affect fit.

    3

    Tell us what it needs to do

    A bedroom and home office? More storage? Keep selected pieces and set your preferences.

    4

    Explore an arrangement

    Add supported furniture and inspect the same room in a top-down plan or 3D cutaway.

    5

    Ask for a change

    Let the assistant prepare edits. See affected objects, fit checks and any compromises before Apply.

    6

    Make it yours

    Refine by dragging or chatting. Undo a change, then export the room project to keep working later.

    02 / The assistant

    Conversation becomes
    a change you can see.

    Moving, rotating, adding supported pieces and changing finishes should all be available through the same assistant.

    “Keep my bed. Add a desk by the window and leave space to open the wardrobe.”

    The assistant works with the actual room data and catalogue. The application checks the proposed arrangement and keeps the final result editable.

    Ask it to highlight a problem, explain a compromise, try another supported item or undo its latest change.

    1. Read the current room

      Dimensions, furniture, openings, locks and your latest edits.

    2. Prepare and check the changes

      Use supported actions; test the resulting room against the available geometry and clearance rules.

    3. Show one coherent preview

      Inspect proposed positions, changed pieces and anything still uncertain.

    4. Apply together, undo together

      A coordinated instruction becomes one reversible change. Newer edits are protected from stale proposals.

    03 / A useful, bounded demo

    The details that
    make the idea work.

    The first goal is a small room with meaningful choices and a satisfying end-to-end flow. The complete engineering notes live in the Markdown.

    Photos help with the setup.

    AI proposes visible furniture and room features. Photos, floor plans and furniture specifications can reduce typing; fit-critical measurements still need review.

    • Estimated values remain visibly approximate.
    • Correct missed objects directly.
    • Use a similar model at the confirmed furniture size.

    A focused visual foundation.

    The proposed app uses React Three Fiber with Three.js, a connected 2D plan and a small curated furniture collection.

    • Free CC0 sources: Kenney, Quaternius and Poly Haven.
    • Adjustable furniture generated from supported parts.
    • Guest workspace with project export/import.
